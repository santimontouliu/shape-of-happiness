An interactive data story · World Happiness Report 2011–2025

The Shape of Happiness

Gallup asks people in 140+ countries to rate their lives from 0 (worst possible) to 10 (best possible). Below, every dot is a country. What separates the left from the right?

Start exploring ↓

01 · The map

Happiness has a geography

The Nordics sit at the top year after year. Much of Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia sits near the bottom. Press play to watch fifteen years go by, or click any country to add it to the comparison.

02 · The explorer

Money matters. But not alone.

Pick a factor to put on the horizontal axis. Bubble size is population and colour is region. The dashed line is the trend, so countries above it are happier than that factor alone would predict.

03 · The drivers

Anatomy of a score

The report splits each score into six measurable factors plus a baseline. Social support and income usually make up the biggest share. Click a factor in the legend to highlight it.

04 · The movers

Risers & fallers

Comparing the early 2010s with the mid-2020s, much of Eastern Europe climbed steadily. Countries hit by economic collapse or conflict fell sharply. Selected countries are drawn in white.

05 · Your turn

Compare countries over time

Uruguay is pre-selected as my personal pick. Add up to three countries with the search box above and see how they compare with the middle 50% of the world, shown as the shaded band.

Method

Scores are 3-year averages of the Cantril ladder question (0–10), labelled by their final survey year. Factor bars are the report's "explained by" contributions, which estimate how much each factor adds relative to a hypothetical lowest-scoring "Dystopia".

Sources

World Happiness Report 2026, Figure 2.1 data (Gallup World Poll); population from Our World in Data; boundaries from Natural Earth via world-atlas.
