The Shape of Happiness

Gallup asks people in 140+ countries to rate their lives from 0 (worst possible) to 10 (best possible). Below, every dot is a country. What separates the left from the right?

01 · The map

Happiness has a geography

The Nordics sit at the top year after year. Much of Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia sits near the bottom. Press play to explore 14 available rankings from 2011–2025 (2013 is absent from the source), or click any country to add it to the comparison.

02 · The explorer

Money matters. But not alone.

Pick a factor’s estimated contribution to put on the horizontal axis, measured in happiness-score points. Bubble size uses the latest available population and colour shows region. The dashed line is a fitted trend, not a causal prediction.

03 · The drivers

Anatomy of a score

The report decomposes each score into six estimated factor contributions plus “Residual”: the Dystopia baseline and the unexplained remainder. Social support and income usually make up the biggest share. Click a factor in the legend to highlight it.

04 · The movers

Risers & fallers

Comparing the early 2010s with the mid-2020s, much of Eastern Europe climbed steadily. Countries hit by economic collapse or conflict fell sharply. Selected countries are drawn in white.

05 · Your turn

Compare countries over time

The default comparison starts with Uruguay, my personal pick, and Finland. Choose up to three countries using Filters above. The shaded band shows the middle 50% of countries with data in each year; every country has equal weight.

Method

Scores are 3-year averages of the Cantril ladder question (0–10), labelled by their final survey year (2025 represents 2023–2025 in the 2026 report). The supplied workbook has no 2013 ranking. Factor bars are the report's "explained by" contributions, which estimate how much each factor adds relative to a hypothetical lowest-scoring "Dystopia".

World averages, medians and quartiles give equal weight to each country with a score; coverage can vary by year. Movers compare the mean of available ranking scores in 2011, 2012 and 2014 with 2023–2025, requiring at least one score in each period. These are averages of overlapping three-year averages. Selected countries remain visible outside the region filter.
